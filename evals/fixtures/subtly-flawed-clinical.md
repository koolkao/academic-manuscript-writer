# Fixture: Subtly Flawed Clinical Draft (False-Negative Adversarial Control)

## Abstract

Among 640 adults discharged after spine surgery, starting physical therapy within 4 weeks improved
12-month pain scores by 2.4 points (95% CI, 1.5 to 3.3; p < 0.001). Patients without anxiety
recovered identically regardless of therapy timing (+0.8 points; 95% CI, -0.3 to 1.9). Early
physical therapy should be mandated for all surgical spine patients.

## Methods

We studied 640 adults who underwent lumbar fusion between 2019 and 2023 and completed their
12-month follow-up survey. Patients who attended physical therapy within 4 weeks of surgery formed
the early group (n = 280); others formed the late group (n = 360). We also fit a spline across
initiation weeks 1 through 14 to find the optimal week. Missing 12-month outcomes were handled with
multiple imputation.

## Results

Early physical therapy improved 12-month pain by 2.4 points (95% CI, 1.5 to 3.3; p < 0.001;
Figure 1), exceeding the 2.0-point individual MCID. In patients with baseline anxiety (n = 250),
the difference was 3.9 points (95% CI, 2.4 to 5.4); in patients without anxiety (n = 390), patients
recovered identically (+0.8 points; 95% CI, -0.3 to 1.9). Roughly half of all fusion patients have
chronic pain.

## Discussion

Our most important finding is that early physical therapy causes sustained pain relief across all
spine surgery practices. There are three key limitations to this study, and these results should be
interpreted with caution.
