# Rapid Point-of-Care Biomarker Panel for Ruling Out Pulmonary Embolism: A Randomized Diagnostic Trial

> Held-out synthetic evaluation fixture (RCT + diagnostic-accuracy design) for Case 11b.

## Abstract

**Objective:** To evaluate whether a rapid point-of-care plasma panel safely reduces CT pulmonary
angiography (CTPA) utilization and accurately diagnoses pulmonary embolism (PE) in primary care
and emergency settings.

**Methods:** In this open-label randomized trial registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05551234,
primary registered outcome: 90-day venous thromboembolism readmission), 400 adults referred to a
tertiary chest-pain clinic (PE prevalence 45.0%) were randomized 1:1 to biomarker-guided triage or
usual care. We report an intention-to-treat analysis of 30-day emergency department revisit rates
and diagnostic accuracy of the plasma panel against CTPA.

**Results:** Biomarker-guided triage significantly reduced 30-day ED revisits (7.4% vs 14.5%;
P = .034) after excluding 38 intervention-arm patients who did not complete the rapid assay. Using
the optimal Youden threshold derived on the trial dataset (cutoff ≥ 420 ng/mL), the panel achieved
94.5% sensitivity (154/163), 88.4% specificity (176/199), and an 87.0% positive predictive value
(PPV; 154/177), confirming its high rule-in accuracy for routine primary care screening.

## Methods

Adults with suspected PE referred to a tertiary chest-pain unit between 2022 and 2024 were
randomized 1:1 (n = 200 intervention; n = 200 usual care) using unsealed block randomization lists
posted in the triage room. Clinicians and outcome adjudicators were aware of arm assignment.
Although NCT05551234 registered 90-day VTE readmission as the primary endpoint, we report 30-day
all-cause ED revisits as the primary outcome here. In the intention-to-treat analysis, 38 patients
assigned to the intervention arm whose plasma sample hemolyzed or who underwent immediate CTPA
before assay results returned were excluded from the intervention denominator (leaving n = 162
intervention vs n = 200 usual care). Diagnostic thresholds were chosen by maximizing Youden's J
directly on the 362 analyzed trial participants, and the same cohort was used to report sensitivity,
specificity, and PPV.

## Results

Of 362 analyzed participants, 163 had CTPA-confirmed PE (45.0%) and 199 did not. Thirty-day ED
revisits occurred in 12 of 162 intervention patients (7.4%) vs 29 of 200 usual-care patients
(14.5%; P = .034; Table 1). At the Youden-optimal cutoff of 420 ng/mL, sensitivity was 94.5%
(154/163), specificity was 88.4% (176/199), and PPV was 87.0% (154/177).

**Table 1.** Analyzed trial arms and 30-day ED revisit outcomes.

| Outcome | Biomarker Triage (n = 162) | Usual Care (n = 200) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmed PE at index visit, No. (%) | 71 (43.8) | 92 (46.0) |
| 30-day ED revisit, No. (%) | 12 (11.2) | 29 (14.5) |

## Discussion

Our randomized trial proves that rapid biomarker triage lowers 30-day ED utilization and delivers
an 87.0% PPV suitable for broad primary care screening. Because the trial was randomized, baseline
differences cannot explain the observed reduction in revisits.
